# **MODULE 1: How Crypto Markets Actually Work**

**Duration:** Week 1 · 4 Lessons · Approx. 60–90 mins total

**Goal:** Students understand what a chart is, how price moves, and what they are looking at before they ever hear about ICC.

## **Lesson 1.1 — What Is Crypto & Why Does Price Move?**

**OPENING**

**💬 SAY:** *Welcome everyone. Before we talk about any strategy, any entry, any profit — we need to understand what we’re actually looking at. A lot of beginners skip this part and wonder why nothing makes sense later. We’re not skipping it.*

**CORE CONCEPT: BUYERS VS SELLERS**

**💬 SAY:** *Here’s the most important thing I want you to take from today. Every single candle on a crypto chart is the result of a fight between buyers and sellers. That’s it. Buyers want the price to go up. Sellers want the price to go down. Whoever has more power in that moment wins that candle.*

**TEACHER TIP:** Use a simple analogy: imagine a tug of war rope. Buyers pull one side, sellers pull the other. The candle tells you who won that round.

**Key points to cover:**

* Bitcoin and other crypto trade 24/7, which means price moves any time of day

* Price goes UP when there are more buyers than sellers (demand \> supply)

* Price goes DOWN when there are more sellers than buyers (supply \> demand)

* A chart is just a picture of that fight, recorded over time

**WHY DOES THIS MATTER FOR ICC?**

**💬 SAY:** *The ICC strategy by Trades By Sci is built around reading that fight and predicting who will win next. When you understand that buyers and sellers are behind every move, the strategy starts to make real sense. We’re going to come back to this idea in every single lesson.*

**HOMEWORK:** Look at a Bitcoin chart tonight on TradingView for free. Just look at it. Don’t try to do anything yet. Just notice how the price moves up and down over time. That’s buyers and sellers fighting.

## **Lesson 1.2 — Reading a Candle Chart for the First Time**

**OPENING**

**💬 SAY:** *Raise your hand — or drop a message in the chat — if the first time you looked at a trading chart it made zero sense. Yeah. That’s normal. Today we’re going to fix that permanently.*

**WHAT IS A CANDLE?**

**💬 SAY:** *Each candle on a chart represents a time period. If you’re on the 1-hour chart, each candle is one hour of price action. If you’re on the daily chart, each candle is one full day. The candle has four pieces of information: the open, the close, the high, and the low.*

**TEACHER TIP:** Share your screen and pull up BTC on TradingView. Point to a single candle as you explain each part.

**The four parts of a candle:**

* OPEN — the price when the candle started

* CLOSE — the price when the candle ended

* HIGH — the highest price reached during that candle

* LOW — the lowest price reached during that candle

**GREEN CANDLE VS RED CANDLE**

**💬 SAY:** *A green candle means the close was HIGHER than the open — buyers won that period. A red candle means the close was LOWER than the open — sellers won that period. The wicks above and below the body show you how far the price went before it came back.*

**TEACHER TIP:** Common beginner mistake: thinking a candle with a long wick is bad. Actually a long bottom wick on a green candle is very bullish — it shows sellers tried to push price down but buyers pushed it all the way back up.

**HOMEWORK:** On TradingView, go to the BTC 1-hour chart. Find 5 green candles and 5 red candles. For each one, write down the open, close, high and low. Post your results in the class chat.

## **Lesson 1.3 — Timeframes: Which One Should You Use?**

**OPENING**

**💬 SAY:** *One of the most confusing things for beginners is that the same asset can look completely different on different timeframes. On the 5-minute chart BTC might look like it’s in a downtrend. On the daily chart it’s clearly going up. Who’s right? They both are. And knowing which one to look at first is a huge part of the ICC strategy.*

**Timeframes explained:**

* Daily (1D) — each candle \= 1 day. Shows the big picture. Reliable, slow.

* 4-Hour (4H) — each candle \= 4 hours. Good for spotting the medium trend.

* 1-Hour (1H) — each candle \= 1 hour. This is where you look for ICC setups.

* 15-Minute (15m) — used to confirm entries once you see a setup on 1H.

* 5-Minute (5m) — used to get precise entries. Not for finding setups.

**SCI’S APPROACH — TOP-DOWN ANALYSIS**

**💬 SAY:** *Trades By Sci uses what’s called a top-down approach. You start on the higher timeframe to understand the trend, then you drop down to a lower timeframe to find and execute your entry. We will cover exactly how to do this in Module 4\. For now, just understand the hierarchy: Daily is the boss, 4H is the manager, 1H is where you do your work, 15m and 5m are your fine-tuning tools.*

**TEACHER TIP:** A good analogy: the Daily chart is like looking at a city from a plane. The 1H chart is like being on the street. You need both views to navigate properly.

**HOMEWORK:** Pull up BTC on TradingView. Look at it on the Daily, 4H, 1H, and 15M charts one by one. Write one sentence describing what the trend looks like on each timeframe. Do they agree with each other? Post your observations.

## **Lesson 1.4 — Support & Resistance: The Building Blocks**

**OPENING**

**💬 SAY:** *Support and resistance are the single most important concept in all of technical trading, not just ICC. If you understand nothing else from this module, understand this: price respects levels. And once you can see those levels, the market starts to make a lot more sense.*

**WHAT IS SUPPORT?**

**💬 SAY:** *Support is a price level where buyers have shown up in the past and pushed price back up. Think of it as a floor. Every time price comes down to that floor, buyers defend it. The market has memory — it remembers where buyers stepped in before, and often they step in there again.*

**WHAT IS RESISTANCE?**

**💬 SAY:** *Resistance is the opposite — it’s a ceiling. A level where sellers have shown up in the past and pushed price back down. Price gets up there, sellers say no, and it reverses.*

**TEACHER TIP:** Show a simple real BTC chart with one clear support level and one clear resistance level. Ask students to identify which is which before you explain it. This makes it stick better.

**How to draw support and resistance correctly:**

* Look for areas where price reversed or stalled at least twice

* Draw the level as a ZONE, not a single line — price is not a laser

* The more times price has respected a level, the more important it is

* A broken resistance often becomes support, and vice versa

**COMMON BEGINNER MISTAKE**

**💬 SAY:** *I see beginners draw support and resistance lines everywhere. Every little bump gets a line. Then they’re confused because price is always ‘breaking’ their levels. The fix: be selective. Only draw levels that are obvious and clear. If you’re not sure whether a level is important, it probably isn’t. Less is more.*

**HOMEWORK:** On BTC 1H chart, draw your support and resistance zones. Try to find just 2 or 3 key levels that are very clear. Screenshot it and share in the class chat.

# **MODULE 2: Market Structure & Trends**

**Duration:** Week 2 · 4 Lessons · Approx. 60–90 mins total

**Goal:** Students can identify uptrends, downtrends, and consolidation without hesitation. They understand the golden nugget rule about trend switches.

## **Lesson 2.1 — Uptrends: Higher Highs & Higher Lows**

**OPENING**

**💬 SAY:** *Quick question for the class: if you want to catch a wave in the ocean, do you swim against it or ride it? You ride it. Trading is the same. You do not fight the trend. You identify it and go with it. Today we’re learning how to identify an uptrend.*

**WHAT IS AN UPTREND?**

**💬 SAY:** *An uptrend has one simple rule: the market is making higher highs AND higher lows. Every peak is higher than the last peak. Every valley is higher than the last valley. That’s it. That’s the whole definition.*

**Uptrend structure:**

* Higher High (HH) — a new peak that is above the previous peak

* Higher Low (HL) — a pullback that stays above the previous pullback

* In an uptrend: you are ONLY looking for buys (longs)

* Do not look for sells in an uptrend — you are fighting the market

**TEACHER TIP:** On screen, mark a clear uptrend on BTC and physically point to each HH and HL as you say the words. Visual repetition is how beginners lock this in.

**💬 SAY:** *Trades By Sci is very clear on this — and I want to be clear too. When you are in an uptrend, your job is to find a good moment to BUY. Not to get clever and try to sell. That comes later, if and when the trend changes. For now: uptrend means buys only.*

**HOMEWORK:** Find one uptrend on BTC or ETH on the 1H chart. Mark every Higher High and every Higher Low. Screenshot and share it.

## **Lesson 2.2 — Downtrends: Lower Lows & Lower Highs**

**OPENING**

**💬 SAY:** *The opposite of an uptrend is a downtrend, and the rules are a mirror image. In a downtrend the market is making lower lows and lower highs. Every valley is lower than the last. Every peak is lower than the last. In a downtrend, you only look for sells.*

**Downtrend structure:**

* Lower Low (LL) — a new bottom below the previous bottom

* Lower High (LH) — a bounce that fails to reach the previous peak

* In a downtrend: you are ONLY looking for sells (shorts)

* Do not look for buys in a downtrend — you are fighting the market

**REAL EXAMPLE**

**💬 SAY:** *Let me pull up a period where Bitcoin was in a clear downtrend and walk you through exactly what that looked like. Notice how every time price bounced, it couldn’t make a new high. That’s the market telling you sellers are in control. As a beginner, your only job in this market is to look for sells.*

**TEACHER TIP:** Use a real BTC downtrend period, ideally 2022 or a recent correction. Students connect better with real assets they know about.

**HOMEWORK:** Find one clear downtrend on BTC or ETH. Mark every Lower Low and every Lower High. Write one sentence explaining how you knew it was a downtrend. Share in the chat.

## **Lesson 2.3 — Consolidation: When to Walk Away**

**OPENING**

**💬 SAY:** *This might be the most important lesson for protecting your money. The ICC strategy does not work in all market conditions. There is one condition where you should put your hands in your pockets and walk away: consolidation. Let’s talk about what that looks like and why it’s a trap.*

**WHAT IS CONSOLIDATION?**

**💬 SAY:** *Consolidation is when price moves sideways. No new highs, no new lows. It’s bouncing between a range. Buyers push it up, sellers push it down, and nobody wins for a long period. This is the market taking a break.*

**Consolidation characteristics:**

* Price moves within a defined range — a top and a bottom

* No clear higher highs or lower lows — just chop

* Fake breakouts are common — price looks like it’s breaking out, then reverses

* ICC setups in consolidation will fail far more often than they work

**💬 SAY:** *Trades By Sci says it clearly, and I’m going to repeat it because I want it burned into your brain: do not trade consolidation. Do not mf trade this. I’m saying the same thing with slightly more polite language.*

**TEACHER TIP:** This gets a laugh and makes the rule memorable. Sci is very direct about this point and students who have watched his videos will appreciate the callback.

**WHY BEGINNERS GET BURNED HERE**

**💬 SAY:** *When you’re new and you see price moving around, it looks like opportunity. Every bounce looks like a trade. And you enter. And then price just keeps going sideways and your stop gets hit. Then you enter again. Same thing. Consolidation is where beginner accounts go to die. The good news: once you can see it, you can avoid it. And now you can see it.*

**HOMEWORK:** This week find one example of consolidation on any crypto chart. Screenshot it and identify the range — where is the top of the range, where is the bottom. Share it and explain why you would NOT trade it.

## **Lesson 2.4 — When Trends Switch: The Golden Nugget**

**OPENING**

**💬 SAY:** *Everything we’ve covered in Module 2 comes together here. This is what Sci calls a golden nugget — and I agree with him. Understanding trend switches is what separates traders who lose money from traders who protect their accounts and eventually grow them.*

**THE GOLDEN NUGGET RULE**

**💬 SAY:** *Here it is: there is NO good reason for price to make a lower high or a lower low in an uptrend — especially on a higher timeframe. If you see that happen, you should be getting ready to switch your narrative. The uptrend may be over. The market may be heading for a downtrend. You should start looking for sells instead of buys.*

**Signs a trend is switching:**

* In an uptrend: price makes a Lower High — it bounces but can’t reach the last peak

* In an uptrend: price breaks below the last Higher Low — structure is broken

* In a downtrend: price makes a Higher Low — bulls are starting to defend

* In a downtrend: price breaks above the last Lower High — possible reversal

**💬 SAY:** *I want to be clear: switching your narrative doesn’t mean you immediately take a trade in the new direction. It means you STOP looking for trades in the old direction. You wait. You observe. You look for ICC confirmation in the new direction before you enter anything. Patience is everything here.*

**TEACHER TIP:** Show a real BTC chart where an uptrend clearly switched to a downtrend. Walk through the exact structural break moment. This is very powerful when students can see it with their own eyes.

**HOMEWORK:** Look at 3 different crypto charts this week. For each one, write down: is it in an uptrend, downtrend, or consolidation? How do you know? Share your analysis in the chat. Be ready to discuss in the next class.

## **Lesson 3.1 — What Is ICC? The 3-Phase Cycle Explained**

**OPENING**

**💬 SAY:** *Alright. This is the moment we’ve been building to. Everything in Module 1 and Module 2 was preparation for this. The ICC strategy is built on one powerful idea: the market moves in cycles. And if you know the three phases of that cycle, you know when to trade and when to wait.*

**WHAT DOES ICC STAND FOR?**

**💬 SAY:** *ICC stands for Indication, Correction, Continuation. Three phases. Three steps. That’s the whole system. Let me introduce each one briefly, and then we’re going to spend a full lesson on each one.*

**The three phases:**

* I — INDICATION: The market gives you a signal. A new high or new low breaks out. This is the setup phase.

* C — CORRECTION: Price pulls back against the trend. This is the trap phase — weak hands get shaken out.

* C — CONTINUATION: Price resumes the original direction. This is your entry.

**💬 SAY:** *Here’s the key thing Sci teaches and I want to hammer this home: you do not enter on the Indication. You do not enter on the Correction. You wait for the Continuation. Most beginners are entering too early. The ICC system forces you to wait for confirmation before you put your money on the line.*

**TEACHER TIP:** Draw a simple diagram on screen: a line going up, then a small arrow pointing back down (correction), then continuing up (continuation). Even a rough sketch makes this click for visual learners.

**HOMEWORK:** Watch Trades By Sci’s Day 1 and Day 2 videos from his course playlist. Come to the next lesson with one question about anything that confuses you.

## **Lesson 3.2 — Phase 1: Indication (The Signal)**

**OPENING**

**💬 SAY:** *The Indication phase is the market raising its hand and saying: hey, something might be about to happen here. It’s a new high forming in an uptrend or a new low forming in a downtrend. It’s structure breaking.*

**WHAT COUNTS AS AN INDICATION?**

**💬 SAY:** *For a bullish ICC, an Indication is when price breaks above the most recent significant high. Not every tiny high — a meaningful structural high. This tells you buyers are pushing through a level that was previously holding them back. For a bearish ICC, the Indication is a break below a significant low.*

**What to look for:**

* In an uptrend: a candle closes above the last swing high — that’s your Indication

* In a downtrend: a candle closes below the last swing low — that’s your Indication

* This happens on the 1H chart or higher for the most reliable setups

* You are NOT entering here. You are noting that the setup has begun.

**WHAT IS NOT AN INDICATION?**

**💬 SAY:** *A candle that barely pokes above a level and then closes back below it — that is not an Indication, that’s a fakeout. A wick above a level without a candle close above it — not an Indication. You need a clear structural break with a candle close for the Indication to be valid. Be patient. Be precise.*

**TEACHER TIP:** Show two examples on screen: one valid Indication and one fake breakout. This side-by-side comparison is very powerful for beginners.

## **Lesson 3.3 — Phase 2: Correction (The Pullback)**

**OPENING**

**💬 SAY:** *This phase is where most beginners either give up on the trade or enter too early. The Correction is when price moves AGAINST the direction of the Indication. After making a new high, price pulls back. After making a new low, price bounces up. This is not the trend reversing. This is the market taking a breath before continuing.*

**WHY DOES THE CORRECTION HAPPEN?**

**💬 SAY:** *After a strong move, some traders take profits. Price pulls back. This is also often a liquidity grab — the market takes out the stop losses of traders who entered too early on the Indication. This is exactly why you don’t enter on the Indication. The Correction would take out your stop. Then the market continues and you’re sitting on the sidelines. Sound familiar? It happens to every beginner.*

**Key points about the Correction:**

* It moves against the trend — that’s normal and expected

* It does NOT break the trend structure — if it does, the setup is invalid

* It can be shallow or deep — there is no fixed percentage

* During the correction you are watching and waiting, not entering

**HOW DEEP IS NORMAL?**

**💬 SAY:** *Students always ask me how far the correction should go. Honest answer: it varies. What matters is that the overall trend structure stays intact. In an uptrend, the pullback should not break below the last Higher Low. If it does, the Indication may have been a false breakout and the setup is invalidated. You walk away and look for the next one.*

**TEACHER TIP:** Use a real BTC example showing a clear correction phase. The more you can show real price action rather than theoretical drawings, the more confident students will feel.

## **Lesson 3.4 — Phase 3: Continuation (Your Entry)**

**OPENING**

**💬 SAY:** *This is it. This is what you’ve been patient for. After the Indication and after the Correction, the market is ready to move in the original direction again. This is called the Continuation, and this is where you enter your trade.*

**WHAT CONFIRMS A CONTINUATION?**

**💬 SAY:** *The Continuation is confirmed when price breaks back in the direction of the original Indication. In a bullish setup: after the pullback, price makes a new move upward and breaks the high of the Correction. That break tells you buyers are back in control. In a bearish setup: after the bounce, price breaks back downward below the low of the Correction.*

**Entry rules for Continuation:**

* Price must break the structure in the direction of the original Indication

* Drop to a lower timeframe (15m or 5m) to find the precise entry candle

* Enter on the break of the Continuation structure — not before

* Your stop loss goes below the Correction low (for longs) or above the Correction high (for shorts)

**💬 SAY:** *Patience is the skill here. If you can just wait for that Continuation confirmation before entering, you will immediately be ahead of 80% of beginners. Most people enter on the Indication and get stopped out in the Correction. You are now trained to wait. That is your edge.*

**TEACHER TIP:** This is a great lesson to do a live chart walkthrough. Find a recent ICC setup on BTC and walk through all three phases in real time, marking I, C, C on screen.

# **MODULE 4: Entries, Stop Losses & Take Profits**

**Duration:** Week 4 · 5 Lessons · Approx. 90–120 mins total

**Goal:** Students know exactly where to enter, where to put their stop loss, where to take profit, and can execute a full ICC trade from start to finish.

## **Lesson 4.1 — Where Exactly Do You Enter the Trade?**

**OPENING**

**💬 SAY:** *This is the question every beginner asks: okay I see the setup, but where EXACTLY do I click buy? That’s what we’re answering today. And I want to give you something most teachers don’t give you — a simple checklist you can follow every single time before you enter.*

**THE TOP-DOWN ENTRY METHOD**

**💬 SAY:** *Here’s how Sci approaches entries, and here’s how I’m going to teach you to do it. First, you identify the ICC setup on the 1H chart. That’s where you see the Indication and the Correction. Then you drop down to the 15-minute or 5-minute chart to find your precise entry. You’re looking for the structural break on the lower timeframe that confirms the Continuation.*

**Pre-entry checklist:**

* Is the higher timeframe (1H or 4H) in a clear trend? Yes / No

* Has an Indication formed? (New structural high or low) Yes / No

* Has a Correction occurred? (Pullback without breaking trend) Yes / No

* Has the Continuation started? (Break back in original direction) Yes / No

* Is there a clean entry on the 15m or 5m? Yes / No

* All five Yes? ENTER. Any No? WAIT.

**💬 SAY:** *Notice that entry comes LAST on that checklist. Not first. Every beginner wants to jump to the entry. But the checklist forces you to confirm everything first. That’s your discipline in a box.*

**TEACHER TIP:** Walk through the checklist live on a chart example, ticking each box verbally. This repetition builds the habit.

## **Lesson 4.2 — Stop Loss Placement: Protecting Yourself**

**OPENING**

**💬 SAY:** *Here’s the honest truth about trading: you will be wrong sometimes. Every professional trader takes losing trades. The difference between traders who survive and traders who blow their accounts is not their win rate. It’s how much they lose when they’re wrong. Your stop loss is what controls that.*

**WHERE DOES THE STOP GO IN ICC?**

**💬 SAY:** *For a long trade (buy), your stop loss goes BELOW the low of the Correction. Why? Because if price goes below that low, your ICC setup is invalid. The Correction broke too deep, the structure is compromised, and the trade is no longer valid. You want out.*

**💬 SAY:** *For a short trade (sell), your stop loss goes ABOVE the high of the Correction. Same logic in reverse.*

**Stop loss rules:**

* Stop goes beyond the Correction extreme — below Correction low for longs, above Correction high for shorts

* Add a small buffer — a few points or a small percentage beyond the level

* Never move your stop loss further away to avoid being stopped out — that is how you turn small losses into account-ending losses

* Once in profit, you can move your stop to breakeven to protect yourself

**THE NUMBER ONE BEGINNER MISTAKE**

**💬 SAY:** *I’m going to say this once and I want you to remember it forever: stop losses that are too tight will destroy your trading. I see beginners put their stop one or two candles below entry because they don’t want to lose much money. Then the market taps that level, takes their stop, and continues in their direction. They were right about the trade but wrong about the stop. Always place your stop where the trade is invalidated, not where you’re comfortable losing.*

## **Lesson 4.3 — Take Profit: When to Get Out**

**OPENING**

**💬 SAY:** *You’ve got a great entry. Price is moving in your direction. Now what? This is where a lot of beginners make their second big mistake. They either get out too early out of fear, or they stay in too long out of greed and watch a winning trade turn into a loser. Let’s talk about how to actually take profit.*

**IDENTIFYING YOUR TAKE PROFIT TARGET**

**💬 SAY:** *In the ICC system, your take profit target is typically the next key support or resistance level in the direction of your trade. If you’re in a long, look for the next significant resistance level above your entry. That’s where price is likely to struggle.*

**Take profit options:**

* Full exit: close the entire trade at your target level

* Partial exit: close 50% at first target, move stop to breakeven, let the rest run

* Trailing stop: move your stop up as price moves in your favor

**💬 SAY:** *My personal recommendation for beginners is the partial exit method. Take half off at a clear target, move your stop to breakeven. Now the worst case is you break even on the trade. The best case is price continues and you make extra. This takes the stress out of letting a trade run.*

**TEACHER TIP:** Greed is the main emotion to address here. Acknowledge it. Say something like: ‘We all feel it. Price hits your target and you think, just a bit more. That’s how winners become break-even trades. Have a plan before you enter and stick to it.’

## **Lesson 4.4 — Using Multiple Timeframes for Entries**

**OPENING**

**💬 SAY:** *We touched on this in the timeframes lesson but now we’re going to go deeper. The multi-timeframe approach is what separates messy entries from clean ones. And clean entries mean smaller stops, better risk-to-reward, and more confident trading.*

**THE THREE-LEVEL SYSTEM**

**💬 SAY:** *Think of it like this: 4H tells you the story. 1H shows you the chapter. 15m or 5m is the sentence where you act.*

**Step-by-step multi-timeframe process:**

* Step 1: Check 4H — confirm the overall trend. Is it up or down?

* Step 2: Check 1H — look for the ICC setup. Has Indication happened? Has Correction happened?

* Step 3: Drop to 15m or 5m — wait for a structure break in the Continuation direction

* Step 4: Enter on that lower timeframe break. Stop below the 5m Correction low.

**💬 SAY:** *What this does is give you a much tighter entry point. Your stop is smaller because it’s based on the 5m structure, not the 1H structure. And your target is still based on the 1H or 4H levels. That means your risk-to-reward gets better. You risk less to make more. This is why learning multi-timeframe analysis is not optional — it is the method.*

## **Lesson 4.5 — Live Trade Walkthrough: Full Setup to Exit**

**OPENING**

**💬 SAY:** *This entire lesson is practical. We are going to take one complete ICC trade from the very first observation to the exit. I’m going to narrate every decision I make and why I make it. Your job is to follow along and ask questions.*

**TEACHER TIP:** This lesson works best if you find a very clean, recent ICC trade on BTC or ETH before the class. Walk through it step by step: identifying trend, spotting Indication, watching Correction, confirming Continuation, entering, placing stop, setting target, and exiting. Narrate your thinking out loud throughout.

**The full process live:**

* Check 4H trend — narrate what you see

* Drop to 1H, identify the ICC phases — label each one out loud

* Drop to 15m, wait for Continuation structure break — point to the exact candle

* Enter — explain why this candle, not the one before or after

* Place stop — explain exactly where and why

* Set take profit target — explain the level and why it was chosen

* Walk through what exit looked like — partial or full

**💬 SAY:** *I want you to notice something important: every single decision in that trade was made before the trade was open. Trend direction, entry level, stop level, target level — all planned in advance. That is the mindset we are building. Not reacting. Planning.*

**HOMEWORK:** Paper trade your first ICC setup this week. Paper trading means you pick an entry, a stop, and a target but use zero real money. Write it all down first: what is the trend, what is the Indication, what is the Correction, where is your entry, where is your stop, where is your target. Track it for the rest of the week.

# **MODULE 5: Risk Management & Position Sizing**

**Duration:** Week 5 · 3 Lessons · Approx. 45–60 mins total

**Goal:** Students understand that risk management is more important than finding setups. They can calculate position size and verify risk-to-reward before entering any trade.

## **Lesson 5.1 — Why Most Beginners Blow Their Accounts**

**OPENING**

**💬 SAY:** *I want to start this module with a hard truth. Most people who start trading lose money. And the reason is almost never that their strategy was wrong. The reason is almost always that their risk management was nonexistent. They found a good setup, got excited, put too much money in, and when the trade went wrong they lost more than they could afford. Today we’re making sure that is never you.*

**THE REAL CAUSE OF ACCOUNT BLOWUPS**

**💬 SAY:** *Here are the four most common reasons beginners blow their accounts. None of them are about the strategy:*

* Risking too much per trade — putting 20%, 30%, even 50% of the account on one trade

* No stop loss — ‘I’ll just wait for it to come back’ is how a small loss becomes an account-destroying loss

* Revenge trading — losing a trade, getting emotional, immediately entering again to win it back

* Overleveraging — using 10x, 20x, 50x leverage on a new account with no experience

**💬 SAY:** *I’ve talked to traders who were actually good at reading setups but still lost everything because of these four things. A good strategy with bad risk management still loses. A mediocre strategy with excellent risk management can still be profitable. Risk management is the foundation of everything.*

**TEACHER TIP:** This is a good moment to be personal if you’re comfortable. Sharing a real experience — yours or someone you know — about any of these mistakes makes it land much harder than just listing them.

## **Lesson 5.2 — The 1-2% Rule & Position Sizing**

**OPENING**

**💬 SAY:** *Here’s the rule that every professional trader lives by: risk no more than 1 to 2 percent of your account on any single trade. That’s it. That’s the whole rule. It sounds simple but most beginners don’t follow it because it feels like too little. I’m going to show you why it’s exactly right.*

**WHY 1-2% WORKS**

**💬 SAY:** *Let’s do the math together. If you have a ․1,000 account and you risk 2% per trade, you risk ․20 per trade. If you have 10 losing trades in a row — which would be an extremely bad run — you’ve lost ․200. Your account is at ․800. You are still in the game. You can recover from that. Now imagine you risked 20% per trade. 10 losses in a row and you have ․98 left. You are done. The 1-2% rule is not about being timid. It’s about surviving long enough to get good.*

**Position sizing formula:**

* Step 1: Decide your risk amount. Account × 0.01 (1%) or 0.02 (2%)

* Step 2: Calculate your stop distance. Entry price minus stop loss price

* Step 3: Divide risk amount by stop distance \= position size

**💬 SAY:** *Example: ․1,000 account. 1% risk \= ․10 at risk. Entry at ․1,000. Stop at ․990. Stop distance \= ․10. Position size \= ․10 divided by ․10 \= 1 unit. Simple.*

**TEACHER TIP:** Walk through 2 or 3 examples with different account sizes and stop distances. Have students calculate their own position size using their actual account size. This personalizes it and makes the math stick.

## **Lesson 5.3 — Risk-to-Reward: Only Take Good Deals**

**OPENING**

**💬 SAY:** *Risk-to-reward is the ratio of how much you risk on a trade versus how much you stand to gain. If you risk ․20 to potentially make ․40, that’s a 1:2 risk-to-reward ratio. The ICC strategy, when done correctly, naturally produces high risk-to-reward trades. But only if you follow the rules.*

**WHY MINIMUM 1:2 MATTERS**

**💬 SAY:** *Here’s the math that changes how you think about trading. If you have a 1:2 risk-to-reward, you only need to be right 34% of the time to be profitable. Let that sink in. You can be wrong on 2 out of every 3 trades and still make money. Because every time you win, you win twice as much as you lose. This is why traders say ‘it’s not about being right, it’s about being profitable.’*

**Pre-trade risk-to-reward check:**

* Measure the distance from entry to stop loss — that is your risk

* Measure the distance from entry to take profit target — that is your reward

* Divide reward by risk. If the result is less than 2, do not take the trade.

* If the result is 2 or more, the trade passes the test

**💬 SAY:** *Think of it like a job offer. Someone offers you a job where you might make ․30 but you definitely have to commute 2 hours each way. You’d say no. But if they offer ․60 for that same commute, maybe you consider it. Every trade is a deal. Only take good deals.*

**HOMEWORK:** Go back to the ICC setup you paper traded last week. Calculate the actual risk-to-reward ratio of that trade. Was it 1:2 or better? If not, where could you have adjusted the entry or target to improve it? Share your calculation.

# **MODULE 6: Mindset, Consistency & Next Steps**

**Duration:** Week 6 · 3 Lessons · Approx. 45–60 mins total

**Goal:** Students develop the psychological foundation for long-term consistency. They leave with a written personal trading plan they can immediately use.

## **Lesson 6.1 — Trading Psychology for Beginners**

**OPENING**

**💬 SAY:** *We’ve covered the strategy. We’ve covered the entries. We’ve covered the risk management. Now we need to talk about the thing that will destroy all of that if you don’t address it: your own mind. Trading psychology is rarely taught in beginner courses. That’s why I’m teaching it here. Because I’ve seen technically correct traders still lose because of their emotions.*

**The four enemies of the beginner trader:**

* FEAR — you see a perfect ICC setup, you’ve done the checklist, and you still can’t click the button. Fear of being wrong keeps you out of good trades.

* GREED — your trade hits target, you want more, you stay in. Price reverses. A winner becomes a loser.

* FOMO — Fear Of Missing Out. You see price moving fast and enter without a setup because you don’t want to miss it. This is how you buy the top and sell the bottom.

* REVENGE TRADING — you take a loss, you’re angry, you immediately enter again to ‘win it back.’ You are now trading with emotion, not analysis. This is how small losses become large ones.

**💬 SAY:** *Here’s the good news: the ICC system is one of the best systems I know of for removing emotion from trading. Why? Because the rules are clear. You have a checklist. You either see the setup or you don’t. You either have 1:2 or you don’t. When the rules are that clear, there’s less room for emotion to creep in.*

**TEACHER TIP:** This lesson benefits enormously from real stories. Share your own experience with any of these four enemies. Students who see that even experienced traders deal with this feel less alone and more equipped to handle it.

**THE SOLUTION: PROCESS OVER OUTCOME**

**💬 SAY:** *Professional traders are not attached to the outcome of any individual trade. They are attached to the PROCESS. Did I follow my rules? Did I check the trend? Did I wait for Continuation? Did I place my stop correctly? If yes, that was a good trade — whether it won or lost. If you followed the process and took a loss, that is fine. It is a cost of doing business. If you broke your rules and won, that is actually bad — because you’re reinforcing undisciplined behavior. Process is everything.*

## **Lesson 6.2 — How to Build a Trading Routine**

**OPENING**

**💬 SAY:** *Consistency in trading comes from consistency in behavior. The most successful traders I know are boring. They do the same thing every day. They look at the charts at the same time. They use the same checklist. They journal every trade. They review every week. It’s not glamorous. It works.*

**A DAILY TRADING ROUTINE FOR BEGINNERS**

**Before your session:**

* Check 4H chart — what is the current trend on your asset?

* Mark any key support and resistance levels that are relevant right now

* Are there any ICC setups forming or already in place?

* Set a price alert if you need to wait for a level

**During your session:**

* Only trade what you planned — no setups that weren’t on your pre-session list

* Use your checklist for every single entry — no exceptions

* Once you’re in a trade, walk away and let it work — stop watching every candle

**After your session:**

* Journal every trade — what was the setup, entry, stop, target, result

* Note how you felt during the trade — were you anxious? Greedy? Calm?

* Score yourself on process: did you follow all the rules?

**💬 SAY:** *The journal is the most underrated tool in trading. Most beginners don’t do it because it’s not exciting. But your journal is where you find your patterns. Maybe you always overtrade on Monday mornings. Maybe you always exit too early on Wednesday afternoons. You will never know this without a journal. Start one from your very first trade.*

## **Lesson 6.3 — Putting It All Together: Your Trading Plan**

**OPENING**

**💬 SAY:** *This is your final lesson and your graduation assignment. By the end of today, you are going to have a written trading plan. This is the document you refer to every single time you sit down to trade. It is your rulebook. Nothing happens without the plan saying it’s allowed.*

**WHY A WRITTEN TRADING PLAN MATTERS**

**💬 SAY:** *A plan you haven’t written down is not a plan. It’s a feeling. Feelings change depending on whether your last trade won or lost, whether you’re tired or energized, whether the market is exciting or boring. A written plan is objective. It doesn’t care how you feel today. It just tells you the rules.*

**Your trading plan must include:**

* Asset: which crypto will you trade? (BTC, ETH, or others you know well)

* Timeframe: which timeframe will you use to identify ICC setups? (1H recommended)

* Trend rule: you will only trade in the direction of the higher timeframe trend

* ICC rules: full checklist — Indication, Correction, Continuation confirmed before any entry

* Risk per trade: what percentage of your account will you risk? (1-2%)

* Stop loss rule: where does your stop go? (Beyond the Correction extreme)

* Take profit rule: where is your target? (Next key support or resistance)

* Risk-to-reward minimum: you will not take any trade below 1:2

* Session hours: when will you look at charts? Set a schedule and stick to it

* Journal: you will record every trade with entry, stop, target, result, and emotional notes

**💬 SAY:** *I want you to write this plan out. Actual pen and paper, or a document on your computer. Not in your head. Then I want you to share it in the class chat so we can all review it together. This is your commitment to yourself as a trader.*
